# Hacking History & Ethics — Week 3 Notes

## What Is Hacking? (Brief History)
- **Origins (1960s, MIT):** "Hack" originally meant an elegant, creative solution to a problem. Early hacker ethic: information should be free, understanding systems is a right.
- **1960s–70s — Phone Phreaking:** Exploiting the phone system's in-band signaling. The 2600 Hz tone could seize control of trunk lines; "blue boxes" generated tones to route calls for free. Famous figure: John Draper ("Captain Crunch").
- **1980s — Personal Computers:** Hacking spread from universities into homes. Notable: Kevin Mitnick, The 414s, the Morris Worm (1988), Chaos Computer Club founded in Germany.
- **1990s — Internet Explosion:** Rise of open-source (Linux, 1991) alongside "script kiddies" using pre-made tools without understanding them. IRC became the main hub for hacker communities and knowledge sharing.

## The Good, the Bad, and the Complex
**Positive impact of hacking:**
- GNU Project (1983) — Richard Stallman, software freedom / copyleft
- World Wide Web (1989) — Tim Berners-Lee released it freely, no patents
- Bitcoin (2008) — Satoshi Nakamoto, decentralized trust-less system
- Snowden revelations (2013) — exposed mass surveillance, sparked encryption adoption

**Malicious hacking:**
- Financial crime (fraud, ransomware, identity theft)
- Espionage (corporate & nation-state)
- Personal harm (harassment, doxing)

## Intention Shapes Everything
Same skills, different intentions:
- **Professional/authorized** → pentesting, red teaming, bug bounties — clear scope, permission, reporting
- **Gray area** → curiosity-driven exploration, hacktivism (Anonymous, WikiLeaks) — unauthorized but not always malicious
- **Criminal** → financial gain, espionage, revenge

## Ethics: The Core Frameworks
| Framework | Core Idea | Key Thinker | Cybersecurity Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deontological | Actions right/wrong based on rules, not outcomes | Kant | Unauthorized access is *always* wrong, regardless of intent |
| Consequentialism | Judge actions by their outcomes | Bentham, Mill | Breaking in might be justified if it prevents greater harm |
| Virtue Ethics | Focus on character (honesty, courage, integrity) | Aristotle | Good character naturally leads to ethical behavior |
| Care Ethics | Focus on relationships and responsibility for others | Gilligan | Consider who is affected and how |

## The One Line That Actually Matters
Underneath all the philosophy, professional/ethical hacking comes down to one practical question:

> **Do I have written, explicit permission to test this system?**

Yes → ethical hacking (pentesting, bug bounty, red team).
No → illegal, regardless of intent or outcome.

## Key Takeaway
Ethics isn't just academic — cybersecurity professionals have real power (access, exploit knowledge, influence over systems), and that power comes with responsibilities: confidentiality, integrity, competence, accountability.
